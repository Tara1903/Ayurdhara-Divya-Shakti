# SECTION 1
# PURPOSE

Colour is one of the fastest ways customers recognize a brand.
Before a customer reads typography, touches packaging, or understands the product, they experience colour.
This document establishes the permanent colour language of AYURDHARA DIVYA SHAKTI.
Every colour decision shall communicate trust, authenticity, wellness and premium craftsmanship.

---

# SECTION 2
# COLOUR PHILOSOPHY

The purpose of colour is communication. Not decoration.
Every colour shall answer one question:
"What information does this colour communicate?"

If a colour has no communicative purpose, it shall not be used.

---

# SECTION 3
# BRAND COLOUR HIERARCHY

Every colour belongs to one of four levels.

### Level 1 — Identity Colours
Permanent brand colours.
Used for:
Brand Logo
Primary Typography
Website Identity
Packaging Identity
Brand Recognition

These colours shall remain consistent across every product.

---

### Level 2 — Category Colours
Different product families may receive controlled accent colours.
Category colours shall assist navigation, not redefine the brand.

---

### Level 3 — Functional Colours
Used only to communicate information.
Examples:
Warnings
Instructions
Certification
Safety
Storage
Medical Information

---

### Level 4 — Decorative Colours
Decorative colours shall remain minimal.
Decorative colour must never overpower brand colour.

---

# SECTION 5
# SECONDARY PALETTE

Secondary colours provide visual flexibility.

Examples:
Olive Green
Muted Sage
Warm Sand
Natural Clay
Muted Beige
Light Stone
Copper
Soft Charcoal

Secondary colours shall always support the Primary Palette.

---

# SECTION 6
# ACCENT COLOURS

Accent colours attract attention.
They shall be used sparingly.

Recommended usage:
Call-to-Action
Premium Seal
Offer Badge
Collection Identifier
Special Edition

Accent colours shall never dominate layouts.

---

---

# SECTION 8
# COLOUR BALANCE

The recommended visual balance is:
70% Primary Colours
↓
20% Secondary Colours
↓
10% Accent Colours

Accent colours shall never exceed their intended role.

---

# SECTION 9
# CONTRAST

Readability is mandatory.
Every colour combination shall provide sufficient contrast.
Typography shall always remain readable.
No decorative colour decision may reduce accessibility.
Contrast shall always take priority over aesthetics.

---

# SECTION 10
# DIGITAL VS PRINT

Digital colours and printed colours behave differently.

The Creative System shall anticipate:
Ink Absorption
Paper Texture
Lighting Conditions
Screen Brightness
Colour Profiles
Monitor Calibration
Commercial Printing

Colour consistency shall remain within acceptable production tolerance.

---

# SECTION 11
# MATERIAL INTERACTION

Colours change according to material.

Examples:
Matte Paper: Softens colour.
Glass: Adds depth.
Amber Glass: Warms internal colours.
Foil: Reflects ambient light.
Textured Paper: Reduces saturation.

The Creative System shall consider material before approving colour.

---

# SECTION 12
# LIGHT INTERACTION

Lighting influences perceived colour.

Preferred lighting:
Morning Daylight
Neutral Studio Light
Soft Window Light
Warm Interior Light

Avoid:
Blue Studio Lighting
Extreme Sunset Lighting
Artificial Neon
Overly Stylized Colour Grading

The brand colour shall remain recognizable under realistic lighting.

---

# SECTION 15
# METALLIC COLOURS

Approved metallic finishes:
Soft Gold
Antique Gold
Brushed Brass
Copper

Metallic finishes shall communicate craftsmanship.
Mirror chrome is prohibited.
Highly reflective metallic gradients are prohibited.
Luxury shall remain understated.

---

# SECTION 16
# PROHIBITED COLOURS

The following shall not become dominant brand colours:
Neon Green
Electric Blue
Hot Pink
Bright Purple
Fluorescent Orange
Acid Yellow
Cyberpunk Cyan
Rainbow Gradients
Artificial Metallic Gradients
Highly Saturated RGB Colours

These colours conflict with the visual language of premium Ayurveda.
